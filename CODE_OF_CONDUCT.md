# Code of Conduct

HyperMind's project spaces should support respectful, technically rigorous collaboration. This policy applies to repository issues, pull requests, reviews, discussions, and other spaces operated for the project. It also applies when someone explicitly represents the project; it does not claim authority over unrelated private activity.

## Expected behavior

Treat participants with respect regardless of experience, identity, disability, background, or viewpoint. Explain disagreements with evidence and address the work rather than attacking the person. Welcome good-faith questions, accept corrections, and respect decisions to end an unproductive exchange.

Be accurate about results and provenance. Do not present fabricated test results, undisclosed harmful payloads, or another person's work as your own. Protect private memory, credentials, and personal information when sharing reproductions or screenshots.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, threats, intimidation, stalking, or discriminatory abuse.
- Sexual harassment or unwanted sexual content directed at participants.
- Publishing private information or credentials without consent.
- Repeated personal attacks, targeted disruption, or retaliation for reporting a concern.
- Impersonation, deceptive attribution, or knowingly misleading others about security or verification evidence.

Technical criticism, reporting defects, and disagreeing with a design are welcome when expressed respectfully. A disagreement alone is not misconduct.

## Reporting and response

For suspected software vulnerabilities, use [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md). For visible abusive content, use the platform's reporting controls. A sensitive conduct report should go through a private contact publicly designated by the repository owners, if one is available. Otherwise, request a private conduct-reporting channel without publishing allegations or personal details. This file does not invent a private mailbox or promise confidentiality from the hosting platform.

Repository owners and people they authorize to moderate may remove content, request a change in behavior, restrict participation, or exclude an account when necessary. Responses should consider severity and repetition, protect affected participants, and explain the decision where doing so does not expose private information. A warning is not required before addressing an immediate threat or serious abuse.

Participants may request reconsideration through the same private channel and provide relevant context. A moderator personally involved in a report should seek review by another authorized person when available. There is no guaranteed response time or independent appeals body.

Do not retaliate against someone who reports a concern in good faith, and do not publish a private report to pressure its participants.
